# Abstract Algebra

## Basic Concepts of Groups

**Definition 1.1.4** (Semigroup and Monoid)

Let $S$ be a nonempty set on which a binary operation $*$ satisfying the associative law is defined; then $\left[S;*\right]$ is called a semigroup. If there exists an identity element $e$ in the semigroup, i.e. $a\in S$ for any $ea=ae=a$, then the semigroup is called a monoid; if the operation also satisfies the commutative law, it is called a commutative monoid.

**Example 1.1.7** (Transformation Monoid)

Let $X$ be a nonempty set and $M\left(X\right)$ the set of all transformations on $X$. Then $M\left(X\right)$ forms a monoid under composition of transformations, with the identity transformation of $X$ as its identity element.

**Definition 1.1.10** (Inverse Element)

Let $S$ be a monoid, $e$ its identity element, and $a\in S$. If there exists $b\in S$ such that $ba=e$ ($ab=e$), then $b$ is called a left (right) inverse of $a$; if $ba=ab=e$, then $b$ is called an inverse of $a$, and in this case $a$ is called an invertible element.

**Definition 1.1.14** (Group and Abelian Group)

If every element of a monoid $G$ is invertible, then $G$ is called a group. If the operation in the group also satisfies the commutative law, then $G$ is called a commutative group, or an abelian group.

**Lemma 1.1.17** (Cancellation Law)

The operation of a group $G$ satisfies the left (right) cancellation law, i.e. for any $a,b,c\in G$, $ab=ac$ ($ba=ca$) implies $b=c$.

**Definition 1.2.1** (Subgroup)

Let $H$ be a nonempty subset of a group $G$. If $H$ itself forms a group under the operation of $G$, then $H$ is called a subgroup of $G$, denoted $H< G$.

**Theorem 1.2.2** (Subgroup Criterion)

Let $H$ be a nonempty subset of a group $G$. Then $H< G$ if and only if $a,b\in H$ for any $ab^{-1}\in H$.

**Theorem 1.2.22** (Lagrange's Theorem)

Let $G$ be a finite group and $H< G$. Then

$$
\left|G\right|=\left[G:H\right]\cdot\left|H\right|,
$$

where $\left[G:H\right]$ is the index of $H$ in $G$. Hence the order of the subgroup $H$ divides the order of the group $G$; in particular, the order of any element also divides $\left|G\right|$.

**Corollary 1.2.23** (Multiplicativity of Index)

Let $G$ be a finite group and $K< H< G$. Then

$$
\left[G:K\right]=\left[G:H\right]\cdot\left[H:K\right].
$$

**Basic Knowledge** (Symmetric Difference)

For any $A,B\in\mathcal P\left(X\right)$, the symmetric difference is defined as

$$
A\triangle B=\left(A\setminus B\right)\cup\left(B\setminus A\right).
$$

**Basic Knowledge** (Order of an Element)

Let $G$ be a group and $a\in G$. Then $\left\langle a\right\rangle=\left\{a^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb Z\right\}$ is a subgroup of $G$, called the subgroup generated by $a$. The order of this subgroup is called the order of the element $a$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Least Common Multiple and Greatest Common Divisor)

For positive integers $m,n$, $\left(m,n\right)$ denotes the greatest common divisor and $\left[m,n\right]$ the least common multiple, satisfying $\left(m,n\right)\left[m,n\right]=mn$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Dirichlet Convolution)

For $f,g\in S$, the Dirichlet convolution is defined as

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;\left(f*g\right)\left(n\right)\\
&=\sum_{d\mid n}f\left(d\right)g\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\\
&=\sum_{ab=n}f\left(a\right)g\left(b\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

## Group Homomorphisms and the Structure of Groups

**Definition 1.3.1** (Normal Subgroup)

Let $H$ be a subgroup of a group $G$. If for any $g\in G$ and $h\in H$ we have $ghg^{-1}\in H$, then $H$ is called a normal subgroup of $G$, denoted $H\triangleleft G$.

**Theorem 1.3.5** (Equivalent Conditions for a Normal Subgroup)

Let $G$ be a group and $H< G$. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1) $H\triangleleft G$;

(2) for any $g\in G$, $gH=Hg$;

(3) for any $a,b\in G$, $aH\cdot bH=abH$.

**Theorem 1.3.6** (Quotient Group)

Let $G$ be a group and $H\triangleleft G$. Then $G/H$ forms a group under coset multiplication $aH\cdot bH=abH$, called the quotient group of $G$ by $H$.

**Definition 1.4.1** (Group Homomorphism and Isomorphism)

Let $G$ and $G'$ be two groups. If a map $f:G\to G'$ satisfies

$$
f\left(ab\right)=f\left(a\right)f\left(b\right),\ \forall a,b\in G,
$$

then $f$ is called a group homomorphism from $G$ to $G'$. If the homomorphism $f$ is a bijection, then $f$ is called a group isomorphism from $G$ to $G'$; in this case the groups $G$ and $G'$ are said to be isomorphic, denoted $G\cong G'$.

**Definition 1.4.9** (Left Translation and Right Translation)

For any $a\in G$, the transformations $G$ on $L_{a},R_{a}$ are defined by $L_{a}\left(g\right)=ag$, $R_{a}\left(g\right)=ga$, $\forall g\in G$. They are called the left translation and right translation determined by $a$, respectively.

**Definition 1.4.11** (Kernel and Image of a Homomorphism)

Let $f:G\to G'$ be a group homomorphism. The sets

$$
\ker f=\left\{a\in G\mid f\left(a\right)=e'\right\}
$$

and $\mathrm{Im}\left(f\right)=f\left(G\right)$ are called the kernel and image of the homomorphism $f$, respectively.

**Theorem 1.4.19** (Fundamental Homomorphism Theorem for Groups)

Let $f:G\to G'$ be a surjective group homomorphism. Then $G/\ker f\cong G'$.

**Theorem 1.5.5** (Classification of Cyclic Groups)

Let $G$ be a cyclic group. If $G$ is infinite, then $G$ is isomorphic to $\left(\mathbb Z,+\right)$; if $G$ has order $n$, then $G$ is isomorphic to $\left(\mathbb Z_{n},+\right)$. Hence two cyclic groups are isomorphic if and only if they have the same order.

**Lemma 1.5.7** (Subgroup Structure of Cyclic Groups)

Every subgroup of a cyclic group $G=\left\langle a\right\rangle$ is of the form $\left\langle a^{l}\right\rangle$, $l\in\mathbb N$, and is therefore also cyclic.

**Theorem 1.5.8** (Existence and Uniqueness of Subgroups of Cyclic Groups)

Let $G$ be a cyclic group of order $n$ and $k\mid n$. Then there exists a unique subgroup of order $G$ in $k$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Conjugate Elements and Conjugacy Classes)

Two elements $G$ in $a,b$ are said to be conjugate if there exists $g\in G$ such that $b=gag^{-1}$. The set of all elements conjugate to $a$ is called the conjugacy class of $a$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Product of Subgroups)

Let $H,K$ be subgroups of a group $G$. Then

$$
HK=\left\{hk\mid h\in H,k\in K\right\}.
$$

$HK$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $HK=KH$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Second Isomorphism Theorem)

Let $A,B$ be subgroups of $G$ with $B\trianglelefteq G$ and $A\le G$. Then $A\cap B\trianglelefteq A$ and $A/\left(A\cap B\right)\cong AB/B$. In particular, if both $A,B$ are normal subgroups of $G$, then $AB/\!A\cong B/\left(A\cap B\right)$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Anti-Homomorphism)

If a map $f:G\to G'$ satisfies $f\left(ab\right)=f\left(b\right)f\left(a\right)$, then $f$ is called an anti-homomorphism. If the anti-homomorphism is a bijection, it is called an anti-isomorphism.

**Basic Knowledge** (Euler's Totient Function)

$\varphi\left(n\right)$ is the number of positive integers coprime to $n$ and not exceeding $n$. For a positive integer $n$,

$$
\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi\left(d\right)=n.
$$

$n$ has exactly $\varphi\left(n\right)$ generators.
